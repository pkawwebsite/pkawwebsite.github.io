Learning for Optimisation: Key Challenges, Insights, and the Road Ahead
Prof. Yi Mei, Victoria University of Wellington, New Zealand

Abstract. Learning for optimisation has rapidly become a vibrant research area at the intersection of optimisation and machine learning. Over the past decade, it has attracted growing attention, with increasing effort devoted to developing machine learning methods that enhance optimisation. In this talk, I will reflect on my own research journey in this field and offer my perspective on where it is heading. I will trace how the area emerged from several distinct research communities, highlight its key design challenges, and outline the open questions that remain. These include designing effective algorithmic frameworks, developing suitable state representations and encodings, and keeping evaluation and simulation both effective and efficient during learning. I will also examine the difficulty of achieving robust cross-domain generalisation and the need for greater explainability and interpretability in learned optimisation models and methods. Together, these issues define both the opportunities and the current limits of learning-driven optimisation.

Bio. Dr. Yi Mei is a Professor and Programme Director (Computer Science) at the School of Engineering and Computer Science, Victoria University of Wellington, New Zealand. He received his BSc (Special Class for the Gifted Young, 少年班) and PhD degrees from the University of Science and Technology of China in 2005 and 2010, respectively. His research interests include evolutionary computation and machine learning for combinatorial optimisation, genetic programming, automatic algorithm design, explainable AI, multi-objective optimisation, transfer/multitask learning and optimisation.

Yi has almost 300 fully refereed publications, including the top journals such as IEEE Transactions on Pattern Analysis and Machine Intelligence, IEEE Transactions on Evolutionary Computation, IEEE Transactions on Cybernetics, European Journal of Operational Research, ACM Transactions on Mathematical Software, and top AI and EC conferences (ICLR, IJCAI, GECCO). He received an IEEE Transactions on Evolutionary Computation and IEEE Computational Intelligence Magazine Outstanding Paper Awards, three GECCO Best Paper Awards, a GECCO Human Competitive Award, and two EvoStar/EuroGP Best Paper Award. He is an Associate Editor of IEEE Transactions on Evolutionary Computation (Outstanding Associate Editor), IEEE Computational Intelligence Magazine, IEEE Transactions on Artificial Intelligence, IEEE Computational Intelligence Letter, and of five other international journals. He is the Chair of the IEEE CIS Evolutionary Computation Technical Committee, Education Portal Subcommittee, IEEE New Zealand Council, and the former Chair of the IEEE CIS Taskforce on Evolutionary Scheduling and Combinatorial Optimisation. He is a Fellow of Engineering New Zealand and an IEEE Senior Member.
